## Fact sheet (confidence tags)

- **[Reported by several outlets] Binance published an incident/security report on Nov 3** and co-CEO Richard Teng spoke to CNBC. Sources: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/03/binance-co-ceo-teng-says-hackers-used-fake-job-interviews-to-steal-6point3-billion-in-crypto.html), [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/11/03/binance-s-usd6-3-billion-hack-began-with-a-fake-job-interview-exchange-says), [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2026/11/03/891324.htm), [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/cybersecurity/2026/binance-reports-hackers-infiltrated-systems-months-before-stealing-6-billion-dollars/).
- **[Reported] June:** a Binance Wallet DevOps engineer searched online for a popular AI coding tool and downloaded a tampered Homebrew installation instead of the real one.
- **[Reported] September:** attackers used fake recruiter job interviews to reach employees.
- **[Reported] About nine days before the hack:** attackers used the laptop of the employee lured by the fake interview to get into the computers of two more software engineers who worked on Binance's wallets.
- **[Reported] Then:** they compromised Binance's internal AI tools and reached the system that approves transactions.
- **[Reported] Oct 29:** they exploited a new Ethereum feature (identified in coverage as EIP-7702) that kept funds moving after Binance paused withdrawals. Blockaid reportedly found 50 signed authorizations bundled in one transaction.
- **[Reported] Teng:** the attackers "spent months building their way in"; it was not one mistake or one breach. He did not blame North Korea himself, but acknowledged the FBI has linked it to the TraderTraitor group.
- **[Reported] Recovery:** Binance says it has replenished about 98% of roughly $6.34 billion in affected assets (earlier figures: 90%, 95%). About $2.85 billion frozen per Teng (about $3 billion per other reports). Replenished is not the same as recovered from the attackers.
- **[Opinion] A CoinEx founder claims the hack could have been prevented** (three weaknesses). Attribute it as his view.
- **[Unverified] Malware details:** Binance has not published them. Don't state any.
- **Note:** the label "spear phishing" is not used in the sources I found; they describe fake job interviews and a trojanized installer.
