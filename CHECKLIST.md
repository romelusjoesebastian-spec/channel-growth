# Per-Video Checklist

Copy this for each long video.

## Before scripting
- [ ] Topic has search demand (search the name/case in YouTube; check suggested searches)
- [ ] 3+ reliable sources collected (court documents, major news, official statements)
- [ ] Original angle identified (what do we add that other videos don't?)

## Script
- [ ] Hook: strongest moment in the first 30 seconds (the $230M video lost ~50% of viewers in its first ~25 seconds)
- [ ] No greeting, channel intro, or "in this video" setup before the hook
- [ ] First sentence states the shocking fact or the stakes; first visual changes within 3 seconds
- [ ] Check retention at 0:30 after publishing; target 60%+ still watching
- [ ] Open loop that pays off later in the video
- [ ] Subscribe ask after a strong moment, one line
- [ ] "Alleged" used for anyone not convicted
- [ ] Names and terms spelled for pronunciation in the AI voice
- [ ] Sources listed for the description

## Production (nextgenstudio)
- [ ] Voice sounds natural; names pronounced correctly
- [ ] Visual changes at least every ~10 seconds
- [ ] Sources shown on screen
- [ ] No AI imagery presented as real photos of real events
- [ ] No graphic imagery

## Packaging
- [ ] Title has searchable name/term in the first ~50 characters
- [ ] Thumbnail: max 4 words, one clear subject, readable on a phone
- [ ] Description: summary, sources, chapters, playlist link
- [ ] Altered/synthetic content disclosure set
- [ ] Added to playlist / series
- [ ] End screen added

## After publishing
- [ ] Matching Short published with link to the long video
- [ ] Replied to comments in first 24h
- [ ] After 48h: log impressions, CTR, retention in TRACKER.md

## Script quality rules (retention first; added after the Binance video)

Lesson from the Binance video: browse traffic arrived but viewers left within seconds. The script opened with a date, delayed its main payoff to ~4:35, and put background before story. Accuracy and attribution rules stay; structure changes.

- [ ] **Hook (first 10 seconds, ~25 words):** the single most surprising attributed fact plus the stakes. No date, no "on the evening of," no "in this video."
- [ ] **Early reveal:** the thing the hook promises is paid off, at least partly, within 60-90 seconds. Never promise a payoff more than 2 minutes away.
- [ ] **Open loop and payoff every 60-90 seconds.** End each section on a question or a reveal that the next section answers.
- [ ] **Story before background.** Explain a term only when the viewer needs it, in one sentence. Market reaction, price figures and comparisons go late or shrink to one line.
- [ ] **Each section starts with the new fact or twist,** not setup.
- [ ] **Sound like a person:** second person ("you"), short sentences, varied rhythm, one rhetorical question per section. Keep attribution in short phrases ("per the FBI").
- [ ] **Cut anything that isn't the story.** Target 8-10 minutes unless the story earns more.
- [ ] **Retention map:** after the draft, list each 60-90 second beat with what makes someone keep watching. Rewrite any beat that has no answer.
- [ ] **Read aloud;** at every 30 seconds ask "would I stop watching here?"
- [ ] **Check the first 30 seconds** of the finished video against the retention graph at 48 hours; log the result in TRACKER.md.

## What a breakout competitor did (Gloam, Oct 30 2026; structure only, do not copy wording)

Benchmark: a channel with ~5.8K subscribers and 2 videos got ~1.75M views in 4 days on the same Binance story (title "How Binance Lost $6,300,000,000 in One Night", 14:54, published ~28 hours after the hack). Size was not the barrier; packaging, hook and story structure were.

- [ ] **Hook = one hyper-specific, surprising detail** (a tiny cost vs a huge loss, an exact duration), not a date or a summary.
- [ ] **Then a roadmap in one breath:** "this is how it happened, who did it, and why X" (open loops in the first 35 seconds).
- [ ] **Background in one idea only** (an analogy the viewer can hold), then straight into the story.
- [ ] **Tell the core event as a timeline with exact times and numbers,** with attribution to named firms ("according to TRM Labs"). Do not dismiss technical detail wholesale; verify it against primary sources and attribute it.
- [ ] **Signpost turns:** "here's the twist," "the detail that matters most," "that should have ended it. It didn't."
- [ ] **Show the debate:** include experts who disagree and what is still unconfirmed. It builds credibility and keeps viewers asking questions.
- [ ] **End with a callback to the hook** and a reason to come back ("when those wallets move, we'll be watching").
- [ ] **Chapters named like questions or story beats** ("One laptop?", "Who did it?", "Where the money is now").
- [ ] **Thumbnail tells the whole story in one image** with labeled characters/objects (e.g. HACKER, ENGINEER, a BINANCE safe), a clear flow, no tiny text.
- [ ] **Speed:** publish within about 24-30 hours of a breaking story when the story has a lot of search demand.
